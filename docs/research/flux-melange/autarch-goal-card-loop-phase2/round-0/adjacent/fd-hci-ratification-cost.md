## Findings

### n1-uncrancher-generalized-to-82x-commitment
- **Severity:** P0
- **Where:** Proposal DONE WHEN ("the confirm-step cost... [is] written into the brainstorm as rulings with reasons") against `docs/analysis/2026-08-13-card-drafter-measurement.md`'s per-repo table (`shadow-work` 455 plans/27 PRDs/13 CUJs; `jeddnet` 38/0/0; `uncrancher` 18/0/7; `ravenous` 8/0/6; `jawnomicon` 0/0/0).
- **What:** The goal treats a single confirm session on `unc-rancher` as producing "the confirm-step cost" that will justify ruling 12's commitment to doing this 82 times. `unc-rancher` is not a representative pick: it is one of only two repos in the measurement with any CUJs to draft `persona`/`pain`/`cuj` from, it drafted 4 of 6 fields cleanly (only `success` and `guardrail` declined — tied for the best outcome in the set alongside `ravenous`), and it was explicitly chosen for external funding stakes ("its card is also the funding page's sales copy"). `jawnomicon`, at the other end of the measured gradient (0 plans, 0 PRDs, 0 CUJs), drafts only 2 of 6 fields and declines the rest — a confirm session there is a structurally different task (mostly writing declines and `needs` questions from a blank repo, not reviewing citations).
- **Evidence:** The measurement's own stated purpose (`docs/analysis/2026-08-13-card-drafter-measurement.md` header) was to test what a drafter can *honestly produce*, not what a human confirm costs — no confirm-cost baseline exists at any point in the cited dependency chain. Extrapolating one number, from the best-resourced repo in a 5-repo sample chosen for material-gradient breadth (not for confirm-cost representativeness), to justify a fixed per-project cost across 82 structurally dissimilar repos is exactly the single-instance extrapolation this lens exists to catch.
- **Suggestion:** Either measure confirm-cost on a second, thinner repo (ideally one resembling `jawnomicon`'s profile — few or no CUJs, no PRD, all-decline-heavy) before writing rulings that bind the whole estate, or explicitly scope the DONE WHEN's ruling language to "the confirm-step cost *for a well-documented, funded project*" rather than letting it stand in for the general case. The goal already treats this as consequential enough to write into the brainstorm as rulings — that's exactly the artifact that shouldn't generalize from n=1 on a best-case sample.

### uncrancher-confirm-is-two-chained-originations-not-a-review
- **Severity:** P1
- **Where:** `product-card-format.md`'s own `unc-rancher` YAML example (`success: {state: declined, reason: "No baseline exists..."}`, `guardrail: {state: declined, reason: "Cannot state what must not get worse without a success metric to bound", needs: "Resolution of success first"}`); measurement table shows `uncrancher` declined exactly these two fields.
- **What:** Confirming `unc-rancher`'s card is being planned as one homogeneous "confirm step," but the format's own reference example shows it decomposes into three distinct cognitive acts: (1) review four drafted-with-citation fields for accuracy (`line`, `persona`, `pain`, `cuj` — the low-cost part), (2) originate a `success` metric from nothing — the field that drafted `0/5` across the entire measurement, i.e. the one thing an agent structurally cannot help with — and (3) originate a `guardrail`, which the card's own text says cannot be done until (2) is resolved. That is a sequential chain, not three independent micro-decisions mk can knock out in parallel or skip around.
- **Evidence:** Direct quote from the format doc's worked `unc-rancher` example: `guardrail.reason: "Cannot state what must not get worse without a success metric to bound"`. R3 (`card-check.py:198-209`) requires both fields resolved (confirmed or declined) before `status: confirmed` is legal, so mk cannot ratify `unc-rancher` while leaving `guardrail` for later — the dependency the card itself documents has to be paid out in the same sitting.
- **Suggestion:** When recording the confirm-step cost, split the timer: minutes spent reviewing already-drafted fields vs. minutes spent originating `success` (and, downstream, `guardrail`). Reporting one blended number obscures that the expensive part is a different kind of work than the cheap part, and that ratio will not be `unc-rancher`'s ratio on a project with fewer or no declined-chain fields.

### r3-all-or-nothing-blocks-incremental-ratification
- **Severity:** P1
- **Where:** `dotfiles/common/.local/bin/card-check.py:196-209` (R3 in `validate()`); ruling 12 in `docs/brainstorms/2026-08-13-autarch-universal-interface-brainstorm.md` ("a signal that fires on 100% of rows carries no information... adoption to be gradual").
- **What:** `status: confirmed` is refused unless every field in `FIELDS` (`persona, pain, cuj, success, guardrail`) is either `confirmed` or `declined` — none may remain `drafted`. There is no `status` value between `provisional` and `confirmed`, so mk cannot confirm three fields today, leave two `drafted`, and finish next session; the whole card is `provisional` (and the plan gate stays shut) until every field is resolved in one sitting.
- **Evidence:** Ruling 12's stated design principle is explicitly about *gradualism* — first-touch, spread over time, never bulk — as the antidote to a uniform, information-free signal. R3 enforces the opposite pattern at the field level within a single card: gradual, interrupted engagement with *one* card's five fields is mechanically disallowed by the validator that gates it.
- **Suggestion:** This may be intentional (the format doc calls partial-drafted confirmation "the rubber stamp this format exists to prevent"), so frame as a question rather than an assertion: is the intended incremental unit "one field, whenever mk has a spare five minutes" or "one card, in one sitting, whenever mk has a spare twenty"? If the latter, DONE WHEN's confirm-cost measurement should record it as "cost of one full sitting," and the proposal should say explicitly that R3's atomicity is a deliberate, accepted floor on session length rather than an incidental validator behavior.

### override-hatch-undercuts-the-cost-being-measured
- **Severity:** P2
- **Where:** `dotfiles/common/.claude/hooks/guard-plan-needs-card.sh:84-91` (override branch); read against the goal's premise that the confirm step is costly enough to need measuring before committing to it 82 times.
- **What:** `AUTARCH_CARD_OVERRIDE="<any string>"` always permits the write and only logs the reason — it is never validated for substance, length, or plausibility (`log_line "OVERRIDE plan=${FILE} verdict=${CODE} reason=${AUTARCH_CARD_OVERRIDE}"` then `exit 0` unconditionally once the variable is non-empty). If the true cost of confirming a card is what this goal's finding #1 above suggests — originating a metric from nothing, potentially 20+ minutes, worse on thinner repos — then any agent under time pressure on any of the other 81 projects has a strictly cheaper path available: set a one-line override and move on.
- **Evidence:** The override's only friction is that a reason must be non-empty (`"I meant to" is not a record`); there is no review of whether the reason is a good one, and nothing prevents `AUTARCH_CARD_OVERRIDE="wip"` from working every time. Ruling 12's "on first touch" adoption model implicitly assumes the confirm step gets paid when a project is touched — but the override makes non-payment costless except for a log line nobody has committed to reading.
- **Suggestion:** Not a call to remove the override — brainstorm decision 3 explicitly wants overrides logged, not forbidden. But the confirm-cost measurement this goal produces should be paired with a statement of how the override's existence is expected to interact with adoption: is override usage itself something that gets tracked and reported alongside "82 confirms happened," so the estate can tell "card discipline is working" from "the log has 81 override lines and 1 real confirm"?

## Verdict
The single biggest risk is structural, not cosmetic: the one pilot chosen to produce a per-project cost estimate is the best-case repo in the material gradient, on the one field (`success`) the measurement already proved is unlike the other five in kind, and the validator's all-or-nothing confirm gate means that cost gets paid in one lump sum with a costless escape hatch sitting right next to it. None of this blocks running the pilot — it blocks trusting the resulting number as the number ruling 12 was written to need.
