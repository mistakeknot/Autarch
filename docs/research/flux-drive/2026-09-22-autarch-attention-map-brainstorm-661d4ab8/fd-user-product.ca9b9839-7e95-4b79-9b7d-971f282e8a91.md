Verdict: needs-changes

### Summary

The map's stated jobs are real, and the rail-as-escalation-queue is sound and matches autarch-01's walk directly. But the design asks for permanent, daily screen real estate for four lenses when only one of them (attention) is actually a daily-walk concern per mk's own CUJs — the other three belong to the rare, deliberate cadence of autarch-03's seasonal reshaping. Worse, the data that would make the map trustworthy on day one (ecosystem placement, core-doc presence, dependency edges) is missing for roughly half the estate, which undercuts exactly the "gestalt and spatial memory" job the map is built to satisfy. The composer's existence isn't argued against BB's own thread interface, and its headline capability (bootstrap-from-rail) auto-dispatches in the prototype in a way that seems to outrun the doc's own dispatch gate. No success or graduation criterion is stated for any of this, unlike the CUJs this design descends from.

### Issues Found

1. **[P0] Map placement and neglect data are real for barely half the estate at launch.**
   Evidence: Open Question 1 — "CanonGraph has an `ecosystem` field, but only 51 projects have rows, and Autarch itself has none." Open Question 3 — "A filename scan on zklw found documents for 20 of 29 named gardens." The prototype's own data comment (`autarch-map-rail-v2.html:91`) admits: "ecosystem/layer placement, dependency edges, beads and Mycroft proposals are ILLUSTRATIVE."
   Failure scenario: mk opens the real (non-mock) map on day one. Half the y-axis (ecosystem band) placements are fallback guesses, the neglect-lens doc rings are dashed/unknown for a third of named gardens, and most of the ~69 "quiet" gardens have no data at all. The map's job #1 is "positions never re-rank, so gardens are found by place, not by reading" — but if the place itself is wrong or fabricated for half the estate, mk cannot build the spatial memory the design is banking on. A map that lies about position (even by omission) is worse than a rail that says nothing, because a rail's silence is honest and a map's wrong-looking-confident dot is not. This is the single biggest risk to "will mk trust it enough to open every morning."

2. **[P1] No success criterion is stated for the map+rail artifact itself, and no graduation test between stages.**
   Evidence: Decision 8 states "Order of work: first a read-only attention and outcomes view, then approval-driven dispatch at T1," but nothing says what would prove the read-only slice earned its keep before dispatch is added. Compare to `docs/cujs/autarch-01-daily-walk.json`'s own `success_condition`: "mk opens the door after a day away and, within about a minute, knows what moved and what waits on them." This brainstorm has no equivalent line for the new artifact — not for the map, not for the composer, not for bootstrap.
   Failure scenario: the read-only slice ships, gets used for a week, and there is no defined signal for whether it worked (e.g., "mk stops opening the raw `bb thread` list for status," or "time-to-orient drops below the door's existing ~1 minute"). Without that, the team (mk) has no basis to decide whether to proceed to T1 dispatch or to cut a lens that turned out to be decorative — the exact "measurable success signal for post-release validation" this review is asked to check for.

3. **[P1] Three of the map's four lenses are seasonal-reshaping concerns, proposed as permanent daily-visible surface.**
   Evidence: `docs/cujs/autarch-03-seasonal-reshaping.json` scopes allocation ("which gardens are fast-layer probes churning... which are misfiled"), dependencies/blast-radius, and neglect/document-drift explicitly to a trigger of "A season boundary... or accumulating friction," with a mental model that reshaping "happens rarely, deliberately." `docs/cujs/autarch-01-daily-walk.json`'s mental model is the opposite cadence: "at ~98 projects the default is quiet... a row earns its place on the walk." Decision 2 in the brainstorm nonetheless assigns all four lenses to the map's v1 jobs and only excludes "seasonal reshaping on the map" (the drag-to-relayout *action*, not the lens *views*).
   Failure scenario: mk opens the map every morning per the walk CUJ, and three of the four lenses (allocation, dependencies, neglect) show information that is only actionable a few times a season. On a normal day these lenses are either ignored (dead screen space, competing with the rail for width) or, worse, scanned out of habit and produce false urgency (a neglect-lens fade that hasn't changed in weeks reads as new information every time it's looked at). This is exactly the "list a queue can't show absence" argument turned against itself: the map's differentiator jobs are real, but binding them to a daily-open surface conflates two CUJs with genuinely different cadences the docs already separate.

4. **[P1] The composer duplicates BB's own thread interface, without an argument for why a second chat surface is needed.**
   Evidence: `autarch-map-rail-v2.html:121-122` — rail items 1 and 2 are literally "Agent is stopped on a question for you" against `bbDev | coordinator` and `bbOps | coordinator` BB threads, with `call: 'bb thread interactions answer pint_a76wipes3c'`. The brainstorm's "Rail" section adds a *third* text box ("styled after Cursor and BB... Changes and new projects are bootstrapped through it") for talking to Mycroft, on top of the two coordinator threads that already exist and already do this in BB itself.
   Failure scenario: mk sees a question from `bbDev | coordinator` in the rail, and has two ways to answer it — click through to the BB thread and type there, or type into the map-page composer, which apparently proxies into a *different* new thread ("I'd open a thread to do that and bring the result back here as a review gate," `autarch-map-rail-v2.html:285`). mk is a "solo operator... who now works inside BB, an agentic IDE of threads" — the composer is asking mk to develop a second habit for talking to the same coordinator, inside a page whose stated purpose was orientation, not another inbox. The doc never argues why routing through a third surface beats "click the rail item, land in the BB thread that's already asking."

5. **[P1] The bootstrap-from-rail flow appears to jump the dispatch gate the doc itself sets.**
   Evidence: Refusal list — "No autonomous dispatch before a one-coordinator claim exists." Decision 8 — "Autonomous tiers come only after a one-coordinator claim exists." Open Question 5 — "The claim mechanism... must exist before Mycroft dispatches beyond T1" and is explicitly unresolved ("bbDev | coordinator and bbOps | coordinator threads already exist" but no claim mechanism is named). Yet the prototype's `data-bootok` handler (`autarch-map-rail-v2.html:309-311`) immediately pushes a new garden into `P` with `a:1` (one agent already assigned) and a live thread, on nothing more than a one-line name+purpose from mk, with no mention of a coordinator claim being checked.
   Failure scenario: the composer's most demo-friendly capability — "+ Bootstrap a new project" — is also the one the doc's own refusal list says shouldn't be possible yet, because the claim mechanism it depends on (to avoid racing `bbDev`/`bbOps` coordinators that already exist) hasn't been designed. If this ships as prototyped, it's either an accidental capability escape from the stated T1 gate, or the doc needs to say explicitly that bootstrap is a T1-approval action distinct from "autonomous dispatch" and explain why it doesn't need the claim (project-onboard for a *new* garden can't collide with an existing coordinator the way in-garden dispatch could — but the doc doesn't make that argument, it just doesn't flag the tension).

6. **[P2] A permanently-rendered full map is in tension with the walk's own "quiet unless attention-worthy" doctrine.**
   Evidence: `docs/cujs/autarch-01-daily-walk.json` — "visibility is opt-in at scale, not opt-out," "rows quiet unless attention-worthy," "Leaving without acting is a successful walk, not an aborted one." The map, per "What We're Building," is "one persistent map beside one escalation rail" occupying the full stage width every visit, painting all ~98 gardens (as grid cells, dots, and lane headers) regardless of whether anything needs attention.
   Failure scenario: on a quiet day where the rail correctly says "Nothing here needs you," the map still renders a full grid of ~98 nodes, axis labels, and (depending on lens) allocation bars or dependency edges — none of which are collapsible or minimizable per the doc. The rail's silence is the "successful walk, not an aborted one" signal; the map has no equivalent quiet state, which risks reintroducing the always-on visual noise the door's row-collapsing design was built to avoid. This is a design gap, not necessarily a build blocker, but the doc should state what "quiet" looks like for the map (e.g., does it shrink, does it default to attention lens with unlabeled dots suppressed further) rather than leaving it implicit in the prototype's opacity rules.

7. **[P2] Pin-nudge semantics are undefined once the underlying graph (Ultan) can move a garden's cell.**
   Evidence: Decision 3 — "the pin is a preference, like `door.yaml` pins... Dependencies, neglect and agents are overlays and never move a node." `docs/cujs/autarch-03-seasonal-reshaping.json` establishes that pace/layer and ecosystem/theme membership are themselves subject to reshaping (world edits, stamped, via Ultan later). The prototype clamps a pin to stay inside its original cell (`autarch-map-rail-v2.html:317`: `Math.max(c.x0+8,Math.min(c.x1-8,q.x))`).
   Failure scenario: mk nudges a garden's position within its cell today. Later, a seasonal-reshaping pass (or Ultan-driven reclassification) moves that garden to a different pace layer or ecosystem band — a legitimate world edit per autarch-03. What happens to the pin? If it's cell-relative, the pin silently "teleports" with the new cell and may now sit somewhere meaningless (e.g., nudged toward "fast probe" now rendered inside "doctrine · slow"). If it's screen-absolute, it could visually contradict the new cell entirely. The doc doesn't say which, and this is exactly the kind of stale-preference bug that erodes trust in a spatial-memory tool.

8. **[P2] No idempotency or conflict handling between rail actions and direct BB coordinator-thread actions on the same item.**
   Evidence: rail items 1–2 mirror live BB coordinator-thread questions (`autarch-map-rail-v2.html:121-122`); the doc doesn't describe how the rail learns an item was already resolved directly in BB (e.g., mk answers `bbDev | coordinator` in a terminal while the map page is open and stale).
   Failure scenario: mk answers a pending interaction directly in a BB thread (their existing habit, per the CLAUDE.md workflow discipline and mk's own "the reason I work in sessions/threads is because we haven't built autarch" admission in autarch-01's evidence). The rail, unless it re-polls promptly, still shows the item as pending and inviting a duplicate "Answer" action. This is a plausible day-one friction point for a tool whose entire value proposition is being trustworthy enough to open first.

9. **[P2] Density/legibility at ~98 gardens is flagged as open but has no fallback default, and first-run experience for a near-empty estate is unaddressed.**
   Evidence: Open Question 6 — "Should labels appear only in the lens that makes a garden relevant... or should cells aggregate beyond a density threshold?" is unresolved; the prototype ships the first option (labels only when relevant), leaving ~69 of the mocked ~98 nodes as permanently unlabeled dots.
   Failure scenario: two unaddressed edges of the same problem — (a) at full estate density, cells with many quiet gardens render as an undifferentiated cluster of grey dots with no way to know what's there without hovering each one (no search/filter is described anywhere in the doc); (b) the inverse case — a newly onboarded or sparsely-populated cell — isn't addressed either: is an empty lane×ecosystem cell shown as blank space, or does it collapse? Neither the doc nor the open questions considers whether density and its opposite (near-emptiness, e.g., right after the "bootstrap a new project" flow places one new dot alone in a cell) need different treatments.

### Improvements

1. **State a success condition for this artifact**, mirroring autarch-01's own `success_condition` line — e.g., "mk opens the map+rail after a day away and, within N seconds, trusts the estate's shape without cross-checking `bb thread list` or door.yaml." Without this, there's no basis to decide the read-only slice is worth its next stage.

2. **Split the four lenses into "walk-cadence" vs. "reshaping-cadence" explicitly**, and default the map to attention-only (or attention + a one-line neglect summary in the strip) on the daily walk, reserving allocation/dependencies/full neglect for an explicit "reshape" mode entered deliberately — matching the doc's own claim that seasonal reshaping is rare and deliberate. This keeps all four jobs without asking three of them to justify permanent screen space against a daily open.

3. **Answer "why not just the BB thread" for the composer** before building it. If the answer is "map-selection-scoped prompts save typing scope," say so explicitly and consider making the composer optional/collapsed by default, deferring to the coordinator threads for anything that already has one.

4. **Gate bootstrap-from-rail on the same one-coordinator claim question as other T1 actions**, or explicitly argue in the doc why net-new project bootstrap is exempt (e.g., it can't collide with an in-flight dispatch because nothing exists yet to claim). Right now the doc's refusal list and the prototype's most demoable feature point in different directions.

5. **Add a stated fallback for ungraphed gardens** (Open Questions 1 and 3) before this becomes a build plan — even a visibly-honest "no data" region (e.g., a fifth "unplaced" band, not a guessed cell) would preserve trust better than silently defaulting into a real ecosystem band.

6. **Define pin lifecycle under upstream cell reassignment** (Ultan-driven or manual reshaping) — does a pin survive a layer/ecosystem change, get cleared, or get flagged for re-confirmation? This is a one-line ruling now, and a confusing bug later if left implicit.
