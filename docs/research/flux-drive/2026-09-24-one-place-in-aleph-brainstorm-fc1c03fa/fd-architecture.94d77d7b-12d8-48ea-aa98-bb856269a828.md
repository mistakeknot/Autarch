Verdict: needs-changes

### Summary
The plugin-not-app shape and the four-owner split are sound, and the host-process mechanism for launching a background service from a bb plugin is precedented in Aleph's own plugin API (`account-pool`'s `experimental_defineHostEntry`/host.ts spawns child processes today), so decision 10's "plugin starts the service" premise checks out mechanically. The two live gaps are: the prior review's P0 "two escalation queues" finding (Mycroft's in-process `DecisionQueue`) is still not addressed by the new bead-based decision design, and the "one `autarch serve`" consolidation quietly merges a stdio MCP process into three HTTP daemons without naming the transport rewrite that requires, then (via decision 15's RPC-panel rule) becomes a hidden dependency of build step 1, which decision 19 was written specifically to keep lean. Everything else is small ownership/state-boundary tidiness.

### Issues Found

ARCH-1. P1: Two decision/escalation queues still coexist, unreconciled. `internal/mycroft/escalate/escalate.go` defines `PendingDecision`/`DecisionQueue`, an in-memory, in-process queue wired into the Mycroft TUI (`internal/tui/views/mycroft.go:50,95`) for dispatch-approval decisions. Decision 3 introduces a second, disjoint decision mechanism — a hub-tracker bead per owed decision — with its own lifecycle (file, answer, close, ruling). The brainstorm's reasons for beads ("decisions have to survive thread rotation... Autarch owns no world state") never mention this existing queue, whether it is replaced, feeds into beads, or continues to run in parallel. This is the same defect the 2026-09-22 review flagged as P0 (fd-architecture F4, "two escalation queues... approval in one won't clear the other") and it is carried forward unresolved into this design, not merely unaddressed by omission — decision 2 explicitly scopes Mycroft to "owns initiative: proposing and claiming work," which is exactly where `DecisionQueue` lives, so the new owner map should have forced this reconciliation and didn't.

ARCH-2. P1: `autarch serve` consolidation crosses a real transport boundary the decision doesn't name. `cmd/autarch-mcp/main.go` documents a stdin/stdout JSON-RPC server (`pkg/mcp/server.go` uses `os.Stdin`/`os.Stdout` directly) — one subprocess per agent invocation, no listening socket. Bigend's daemon (`internal/bigend/daemon`, port 8100), Gurgeh's server (`internal/gurgeh/cli/commands/serve.go`, port 8091) and Signals (`internal/signals/cli/serve.go`, port 8092) are long-running HTTP/WebSocket servers. Decision 10 says these "four separate servers... consolidate into one `autarch serve`" as if it's a uniform merge, but folding a per-call stdio protocol into a persistent multiplexed HTTP daemon is a protocol bridge, not a `cobra.AddCommand` exercise — agents that spawn `autarch-mcp` as a subprocess today would need a different invocation path entirely (talk to the running `autarch serve` over HTTP/a local socket, or keep a stdio shim that proxies to it). Nothing in decision 10 or the "engineering defaults" in decision 15 names this as work; it reads as if merging cobra subcommands is the whole job.

ARCH-3. P1: Build step 1 is not actually independent of the server-consolidation work in decision 10. Decision 19 cut focus specifically to keep step 1 lean ("Build step 1 is decisions only"), and decision 7 frames it as "Home on beads first" — the intended fast, low-risk slice. But decision 15 says "panels kept thin over RPC," and the intro states the plugin's host part starts `autarch serve`, which is *the* Go engine — there's no separate "decisions-only" service named anywhere. If the decisions-inbox panel talks to the Go side over RPC at all (as decision 15 implies uniformly for all panels), it depends on however much of `autarch serve` exists at that point, which per decision 10's framing is the whole four-way consolidation, including the MCP transport bridge in ARCH-2. The document never states whether `autarch serve` is built minimally (just enough surface for decisions/rulings) and grown per step, or whether the full consolidation is a prerequisite before step 1 can land. Given decision 19 explicitly re-scoped step 1 to avoid "ornate" scope, this gap should be closed explicitly (e.g., "step 1's RPC surface is decisions/rulings only; bigend/gurgeh/signals/mcp consolidation is not on the critical path for step 1") or the build-order independence claim doesn't hold.

ARCH-4. P2: "Autarch owns no world state" (decision 3's rationale) is asserted again without reconciling existing local state. `internal/door/preferences.go` persists `WorkbenchPreference` records (outcome, task identity, target, account label, draft, recovery handoff) to `~/.autarch/preferences.json`, and `internal/door/display.go` holds related display state — the same files the 2026-09-22 review cited for this exact contradiction (F1: "the design rule 'Autarch holds no state' is contradicted by existing code"). This brainstorm could resolve it cheaply by drawing the line explicitly (e.g., "per-machine UI preference is not world state; decisions/rulings are") but doesn't, so the boundary a reader is meant to plan against is still implicit.

ARCH-5. P2: The feed hook (decision 17) sits in Clavain's owned lane but performs Autarch's job. Decision 2 assigns "Clavain owns policy: routing, review and gates" and "Autarch owns attention: presenting the estate and recording mk's rulings." Decision 17's `UserPromptSubmit` hook injects "the rulings relevant to that project" (focus changes, new rulings, answers to this thread's decisions) at the start of every turn — that's attention delivery, Autarch's stated job, implemented as a Clavain hook. This may be the right call mechanically (Clavain already owns the hook surface CLAUDE.md documents, e.g. `hooks/auto-stop-actions.sh`), but the four-owner framing in decision 2 doesn't say hooks are an exception, so a planner reading decision 2 alone would not expect Clavain-owned code to be doing Autarch-owned presentation work. Worth one sentence clarifying that Clavain owns the hook *mechanism* while the content/policy of what gets surfaced is Autarch's, so ownership of bugs/changes in that hook is unambiguous later.

ARCH-6. P2: Lattice picks up a new trust-verification responsibility that decision 11 doesn't name as scope growth. Decision 16 says "Lattice and the hooks treat unsigned files as proposed," which means Lattice must parse the ratification block, know the `allowed_signers` file/location, and verify `ssh-keygen -Y sign` signatures to decide proposed-vs-ruled status — a capability with real engineering weight (key rotation via `valid-after`, cross-machine signer distribution). Decision 11's "grow Lattice to meet the Ultan thesis" describes indexing/finding-aid growth; signature verification is a different kind of capability (trust enforcement) that should be called out explicitly as new Lattice scope, especially since Lattice is a separate repo (`interverse/lattice`) not otherwise touched by owners Clavain/Mycroft/Autarch in decision 2's split — it's unclear who implements this in Lattice.

### Improvements

ARCH-7. Aleph's plugin API already supports the "host starts a background service" pattern decision 10 relies on — `~/projects/Aleph/plugins/account-pool/src/host.ts` uses `experimental_defineHostEntry` from `@get-bb/plugin-sdk/host` to spawn and manage a child process from the plugin's host side. This is good evidence the "no fork patches" refusal in decision 10/the onepager is achievable, and should be cited directly in the plan instead of re-derived.
